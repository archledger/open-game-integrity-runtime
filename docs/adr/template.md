## Context

Describe the problem, current state, constraints, and why a durable decision is
needed.

## Decision drivers

List the security, privacy, compatibility, operational, licensing, and
maintainability forces that shape the choice.

## Options considered

For each viable option, record its benefits, costs, security properties, and
reason for selection or rejection. A Rejected ADR keeps this analysis instead
of deleting history.

## Decision

State the selected decision precisely, including its scope and explicit
non-goals.

## Consequences

Record positive consequences, negative consequences, follow-up obligations,
and assumptions that remain outside the decision.

## Threat-model impact

Identify affected attacker classes, assets, trust boundaries, threats, and
residual risks. If none change, explain why.

## Privacy impact

Identify new or changed claims, identifiers, retention, logging, or disclosure.
If none change, explain why.

## Dependency and license impact

Identify new dependencies, trusted-computing-base changes, maintenance risk,
transitive exposure, and license-boundary effects. If none change, explain why.

## Validation

List the tests, prototypes, review evidence, and operational checks required to
show the decision works and fails safely.

## Rollback

Describe how to disable or reverse the decision safely. If reversal requires a
superseding ADR or migration, say so explicitly.

## Primary sources

Link authoritative specifications, upstream documentation, or source code used
to make the decision. Explain why none apply when the decision is wholly
project-internal.
